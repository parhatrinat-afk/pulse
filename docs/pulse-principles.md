# Pulse Principles

## Trust

1. Trust before insight.
2. A number must be traceable to its source facts and calculation rule.
3. Imports must be recoverable; an accidental finalize action must not permanently trap bad data.
4. Wrong periods, partial reports, duplicate imports, and unexpected coverage should be detectable.

## Human decision-making

5. Pulse presents facts, changes, comparisons, and transparent calculations.
6. Pulse does not own operational conclusions or business decisions.
7. Human-authored business context can explain known conditions such as resignations, campaigns, closures, or deliberate changes in product strategy.
8. A persistent negative value is not automatically an unresolved problem.
9. Pulse should not repeatedly nag about a condition humans have explicitly contextualized or acknowledged.
10. Pulse has no personality and should not create the impression that it is a manager or decision-maker.

## Product experience

11. Normal navigation follows user goals: Overview → Performance → Reports → Imports → Settings.
12. Complexity should live one level deeper than routine use.
13. Silence can mean success; all-green dashboards and constant reassurance are unnecessary.
14. Existing functionality is preserved until real-world use provides evidence that it should change or disappear.
15. The workbook should tell the same story as the application.

## Architecture

16. Facts and calculated metrics are separate concepts.
17. KPI definitions are centrally registered rather than independently recreated on each report.
18. Reporting categories are independent of source-system category structures.
19. Source systems and restaurant names must not be hard-coded into the neutral base product.
20. Business domains can be enabled independently; disabled or unavailable domains must not affect the presented performance impression.
